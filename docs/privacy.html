HARC privacy policy

Effective date: 13 October 2026

This policy explains how the iPhone app HARC handles information. HARC appears on the App Store as "Hermes Agent RC." Ryan Tucker, an individual developer, publishes HARC. In this policy, "we," "us," and "our" mean Ryan Tucker, and "you" means a person who uses HARC, visits its website, or contacts us about HARC.

Summary

  • HARC sends nothing to us. We operate no server for the app. The app contains no analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash-reporting software, and no third-party libraries or software development kits. Apart from its own code, it uses only Apple's frameworks.
  • Your content goes to the Hermes Agent installation you connect to. HARC is a remote control for Hermes Agent, software that runs on a computer that you, or someone you choose, operates. Your messages and files travel from your iPhone to that installation. What happens there, including whether the installation sends them on to an AI provider, depends on how it is set up, and this policy does not cover it.
  • Your iPhone sometimes contacts other parties directly, mainly when a reply from your agent points to an image, video, or file at an internet address. Section 4 describes each case.
  • HARC stores some information on your iPhone, including sign-in credentials and recent conversation content. Section 5 says what, where, and for how long.
  • We receive information about you only if you contact us or use the HARC repository on GitHub. Apple also collects information about HARC and gives us reports and App Store reviews. Section 2 explains both.
  • We do not sell personal information, use it for targeted advertising, or use it to train artificial intelligence models.

1. What this policy covers

This policy covers the HARC app, the HARC website at https://ryantuc.github.io/hermes-agent-remote-control/, and your communications with us about HARC.

It does not cover how anyone else handles information, even when you reach them through HARC. In particular, it does not cover how these parties handle information:

  • the Hermes Agent installation you connect to, and the computer it runs on;
  • any AI provider, speech-to-text service, or other service that installation uses;
  • any website, file host, or online service your iPhone contacts through HARC, including services you sign in to;
  • Apple, which makes iOS and runs the App Store and TestFlight;
  • GitHub, which hosts the HARC website and its public repository; and
  • Google, which provides the email service we use for support.

Apart from your own installation, each of these parties handles information under its own terms and privacy policy, which we encourage you to read. We do not control these parties. Except for the service providers we use (section 2), we are not responsible for their practices.

HARC is offered only on the United States App Store, and this policy is written for people in the United States.

2. Information we receive

Through the app

The app sends us no information. It has no account system, and it connects to no server we operate. We cannot see your conversations, your files, your credentials, or the addresses of your computers. Apart from Apple's reports (below), we learn nothing about how you use the app.

The app shares no user data with any third party on our behalf, such as an analytics tool, advertising network, or software development kit. If that ever changes, we will update this policy first and require each recipient to protect the data at least as well as this policy does.

When you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address, any name your email shows, your message and the technical details in its headers, and anything you attach. Google provides our email service.

If you open an issue or post a comment in the HARC repository on GitHub, your post is public, and we can see your GitHub username and what you post. GitHub also shows us the usernames of people who star, watch, or fork the repository, and totals of views of the repository and of the sites that referred them.

Please do not send us, or post publicly, passwords, tokens, or the content of your conversations. HARC's connection log, which our support page asks for, can show your computer's address and file paths on it. Read it before you send it, and send it by email rather than posting it on GitHub.

From Apple

Apple collects some information about HARC under Apple's own terms and privacy policy, and gives app developers reports based on it. For HARC, those reports can include:

  • totals of App Store views and downloads, and of use of the app by people who share analytics with app developers, which do not name you;
  • crash reports and diagnostic information from people who have chosen, in their iPhone settings, to share analytics with app developers;
  • ratings and reviews posted on the App Store, with the reviewer's nickname; and
  • if you test HARC through TestFlight, your name, your email address, and any feedback or screenshots you send through TestFlight.

From the website

The HARC website is a set of static pages that GitHub hosts. The pages set no cookies and load no analytics, advertising, or third-party scripts. GitHub receives your IP address and the other information your browser sends when you visit, and handles it under GitHub's privacy statement. GitHub does not tell us who visits the website.

How we use it

We use the information described in this section only to:

  • respond to you and support HARC;
  • find and fix problems in HARC and improve it;
  • comply with the law and respond to lawful requests; and
  • protect the rights, safety, and property of HARC's users, us, and others.

When we disclose it

We disclose personal information only:

  • to service providers that store, carry, or process it for us, such as Google for our email, GitHub for the website and the HARC repository, and the file-storage and software tools, including AI assistants, that we use to answer you and fix HARC;
  • when we believe the law or legal process requires it, or when we need to disclose it to protect anyone's rights, safety, or property;
  • to a person or organization that takes over HARC, including through a sale or bankruptcy, and we will require them to handle it as this policy describes; and
  • with your consent.

We do not sell personal information, share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, or use it for targeted advertising or profiling. We do not collect, use, or sell personal information to train artificial intelligence models, including large language models.

How long we keep it

We keep emails you send us for as long as they help us answer you, support HARC, or keep a record of our exchange, or as the law requires. We set no fixed period. If you ask us to delete emails you sent us, we will, unless the law requires us to keep them. What you post on GitHub stays public until it is deleted there. Apple decides how long its reports remain available to us.

3. How HARC works with your Hermes Agent installation

HARC connects to the Hermes Agent installation at the address you enter, using the credential you enter. Through that connection, HARC sends:

  • your messages, and the photos, videos, and files you attach;
  • your answers to the agent's questions and approval requests, including any password or secret value, such as an API key, that you type when the agent asks for one;
  • changes you make in HARC to your installation's settings, such as its default and fallback AI models; and
  • recordings of your voice, when you use the Hermes dictation engine (section 6).

HARC sends your messages and files only to your installation, never directly to an AI provider. Your installation decides what happens next. Depending on its configuration, it may send your messages, attachments, dictation recordings, and the results of its tools to an AI provider or another service. Those results can include the contents of files on the computer it runs on. HARC does not choose those services for you, does not store your credentials for them, and cannot see what your installation sends them. Your arrangements with those services govern how they handle your information.

The connection's security depends on how it is set up. Over HTTPS, the connection is encrypted. Over plain HTTP, it is not: anyone who can observe the network can read your messages and your credential. If you enter an address without "https://", HARC uses plain HTTP for a local, private-network, or Tailscale address, and HTTPS for any other address. We recommend HTTPS, or an encrypted connection such as a VPN.

4. When your iPhone contacts other parties

Images, videos, and files at internet addresses

A reply from your agent can point to an image, video, or other file at an internet address instead of on your computer. Your iPhone then requests the file directly from the host at that address:

  • Images: HARC requests an image automatically when the reply that contains it appears on screen. The Artifacts screen also requests the images it finds in your 15 most recent conversations as their tiles appear.
  • Videos: HARC asks the host for a video's size when the reply appears, and downloads the video only when you open it.
  • Other files: HARC downloads them only when you tap them.

The host receives your IP address and the information an iPhone app's web request normally carries, which usually includes the app's internal name and build number and the version of the iPhone's networking software. HARC does not attach your Hermes Agent token or password to these requests. If an address is on the same host as your installation, iOS may also send that host the login cookie described in section 5. HARC uses iOS's standard settings for these requests. A host can therefore store a cookie in HARC and receive it back the next time your iPhone requests something from it, and iOS may keep copies of the responses in HARC's cache. HARC stops downloading an image or other file once it passes 32 MB, and a video once it passes 100 MB.

Your agent and the tools you give it choose these addresses. We do not choose them, and we do not see them.

Services your agent asks you to sign in to

Your installation can ask you to connect an account, such as an email or calendar account. If you tap Sign in, HARC opens the secure (https) address your installation supplies, in Apple's web sign-in sheet, after iOS asks your permission. The sign-in sheet can use cookies that Safari holds, so a site you are signed in to in Safari may recognize you. What you enter in the sheet goes to the site that serves the page. HARC cannot see it.

Links

When you tap a link in a conversation, iOS opens it, usually in Safari or in the app that handles that kind of link. Links and images you open from the Artifacts screen open in an in-app Safari view. HARC's About and Home screens link to this website and to GitHub. Each site you visit handles your information under its own policy.

Apple and your network provider

Apple, as the maker of iOS, may collect information about your iPhone and its use under Apple's privacy policy and your iPhone's settings. Your mobile carrier or internet provider handles your network traffic under its own policy.

5. What HARC stores on your iPhone

Credentials. HARC stores the token or password you enter for each backend in the iOS Keychain. It marks each item as available only after you first unlock your iPhone, and only on that iPhone, so it does not sync through iCloud. If you sign in with a username and password, your installation returns a login cookie, which HARC keeps in its own cookie storage rather than the Keychain. Removing a backend in HARC, or signing out of it, deletes its credential and its login cookie. iOS may keep Keychain items after you delete the app, so remove your backends in HARC first if you want their credentials deleted.

Backend details and preferences. HARC's settings hold each backend's name, address, sign-in method, and username; the project folders and conversations you recently used or pinned; the AI models you recently chose; and your preferences. iOS includes these settings, and HARC's cookie storage, in iCloud and computer backups of your iPhone.

Recent conversation content. So that it can restore an interrupted conversation, HARC keeps a copy of recent conversation content in its cache folder. The copy can include your messages, the agent's replies and reasoning, the agent's tool activity, and images embedded in messages. Tool activity can include the contents of files on your computer. iOS protects these files until you first unlock your iPhone after it starts, and does not include the cache folder in backups. HARC keeps at most eight copies, 32 MB in all and 8 MB for any one, and stops using a copy after 24 hours. HARC deletes an expired copy the next time it saves one, and deletes a backend's copies when you remove that backend or sign out of it. iOS may also clear the cache folder when storage runs low, and deleting the app removes it.

Downloaded videos. HARC saves a video you open in its cache folder and reuses that copy if you open the video again. HARC does not delete these files itself. iOS may delete them when storage runs low, and deleting the app removes them.

Temporary files. Dictation recordings, files you preview, and videos you attach pass through temporary storage. HARC deletes each one when it finishes with it. If HARC closes before then, the file stays until iOS clears temporary storage.

Diagnostics. HARC keeps a log of up to 400 connection events and a log of screen-layout events. HARC masks the credentials it recognizes before it adds an entry to the connection log, but the log can still show your computer's address, file paths on it, and error messages from your installation. Both logs stay in the app's memory and are not saved to storage. You can view, copy, or share them from the Diagnostics screen, and they then go wherever you send them. HARC also writes some technical events, which contain no conversation content, to the iPhone's system log, which iOS manages. HARC sends none of these logs to us.

6. Voice, photos, notifications, and other features

Voice chat and on-device dictation. These use speech recognition on your iPhone. HARC requires on-device recognition and will not start them if iOS cannot provide it. Your audio stays on your iPhone. Only the recognized text goes, as a message, to your installation. On-device dictation is the default.

Hermes dictation. If you choose the Hermes dictation engine, HARC records your voice to a temporary file, sends the recording to your installation to be transcribed, and then deletes the file. Your installation may send the recording to a speech-to-text provider, depending on its configuration.

Read aloud. HARC reads replies aloud with your iPhone's built-in voices.

Photos and files you attach. HARC sends what you attach to your installation. It converts photos in formats your installation does not accept, such as the iPhone's default HEIC format, to JPEG or PNG on your iPhone first. HARC does not remove the information embedded in photos and files you choose, which can include where a photo was taken. HARC receives only the photos and videos you pick. It never asks for permission to read your photo library.

Saving, sharing, and copying. Saving an image or video adds it to your Photos library with add-only access. If you use iCloud Photos, it also syncs there. Sharing sends it through the iOS share sheet to the destination you choose. Copying puts it on the iPhone's clipboard, where an app you paste into can read it, and iOS may make it available on your other Apple devices through Universal Clipboard. HARC reads the clipboard only when you paste.

Face ID. HARC asks for Face ID, or your passcode, before you choose "Always allow" for an agent action and before you approve an action from the Dynamic Island. iOS tells HARC only whether the check succeeded.

Notifications. HARC creates notifications on your iPhone from its own connection to your installation. No push service is involved. Notifications contain fixed wording and never the command, question, or value that a request concerns. HARC asks for permission to send notifications the first time it connects to a backend.

Live Activities. Live Activities are off unless you turn them on. When they are on, the Lock Screen can show a conversation's title, which may be the beginning of your first message in that conversation, and the conversation's progress. The expanded Dynamic Island can show the command that an approval request concerns. Anyone who can see your iPhone's screen, or another device that shows your iPhone's Live Activities, may see this information. You can turn Live Activities off, or limit what the Lock Screen shows, in HARC's settings.

Demo mode. The demo makes no network connections and saves no conversation. Settings you change while the demo runs are kept.

7. Permissions

HARC asks for iOS permissions only for the features that use them: the camera, the microphone, speech recognition, Face ID, the local network, adding to your photo library, and notifications. You can decline any permission, or turn it off later in iOS Settings. The features that need it will then stop working, and the rest of HARC will keep working. But without local network access, HARC cannot reach an installation on your local network.

8. Tracking and Do Not Track signals

HARC does not track you across apps or websites owned by others, does not use your iPhone's advertising identifier, and contains no third-party library that does. Other parties may still collect information about your activities over time and across apps and websites when you use HARC or its website. They include the hosts and sites your iPhone contacts, Apple, and your network provider (section 4); GitHub, when you visit the website (section 2); and the AI providers and other services your Hermes Agent installation uses (section 3). We do not control what they collect. Because we do not track you through HARC or its website, neither changes its behavior in response to a Do Not Track or similar browser signal.

9. Your choices

  • Information on your iPhone. Remove a backend in HARC to delete its credential, its login cookie, and its stored conversation copies. Then delete the app to remove what HARC keeps in its own storage. Deleting the app does not remove items you saved to Photos or shared, entries in the iPhone's system log, which iOS manages, or copies in backups of your iPhone made before you deleted the app.
  • Your Lock Screen. Turn off Live Activities, or the Lock Screen option, in HARC's settings.
  • Your voice. Use on-device dictation to keep your audio on your iPhone.
  • Information others hold. Manage information held by your Hermes Agent installation, or by the services it uses, in those systems. We have no access to them.
  • Emails you send us. If you want us to delete emails you sent us, tell us at the address in section 14. You can edit or delete what you post on GitHub through GitHub.
  • Withdrawing consent. You can withdraw a permission you gave HARC at any time in iOS Settings (section 7). HARC asks for no other consent.

10. Children

HARC is a tool for adults who use Hermes Agent. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18. If someone under 18 sends us personal information, for example in an email, we will delete it once we have answered.

11. Security

HARC stores the tokens and passwords you enter in the iOS Keychain, protects its conversation copies with iOS file protection, masks the credentials it recognizes in its diagnostics, and uses no third-party libraries. These measures reduce risk but do not remove it. No app, network, or storage system is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee the security of information on your iPhone, on your computers, or in transit. You control the security of your iPhone and the networks you use, and you or its operator control the security of your Hermes Agent installation.

12. Changes to this policy

We may change this policy. Before a change takes effect, we will post the revised policy at this address with a new effective date. If the change is material, we will also describe it at the top of the revised policy. If it concerns how the app handles information, we will also describe it in the release notes for the first version of HARC that it affects. A material change will not apply, without your consent, to information we received before it. We keep a copy of every version of this policy.

13. What this policy is

This policy describes our practices, and we follow it. It is not a contract and does not create contractual rights or obligations. It does not limit any right you have under law.
